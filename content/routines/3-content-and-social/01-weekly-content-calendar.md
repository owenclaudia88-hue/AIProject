# Weekly Content Calendar

Turns "I should post more" into seven specific things to post, every Friday.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Runs** | Fridays, 14:00 |
| **Cron** | `0 14 * * 5` |
| **Connectors** | Gmail. Google Drive if you keep a content doc |
| **Takes** | One run a week |

---

## Why the calendar is the hard part

Almost nobody stops posting because writing is hard. They stop because on Monday morning they have no idea what to post, and deciding costs more energy than writing does.

This routine takes the deciding away. Every Friday afternoon it hands you next week's seven slots, already filled, each one with an angle and an opening line. You are never starting from a blank page, and you are never posting the same thought three weeks running — because it keeps track of what it suggested before.

It writes openings, not finished posts. Finished posts written by a machine sound like they were, and your audience can tell.

## Set it up

1. **New routine**, name it `Weekly Content Calendar`
2. Paste the instructions and **fill in the bottom section properly** — this is the one that decides whether the output is usable
3. **Schedule** → **Weekly** → Friday, 14:00
4. **Connectors**: Gmail. Add Google Drive if you want it to read and update a running content doc
5. **Create**, then **Run now** to get next week straight away

## Instructions

```
Plan next week's content.

Produce one idea for each of the seven days, Monday to Sunday, using the
weekly shape below.

For each day give:
- the format (short post, long post, video, carousel, email — whatever
  the shape below says for that day)
- the angle in one sentence, written for me, not for the audience
- the opening line, written for the audience, no more than 20 words
- the one thing the piece should leave the reader believing
- an optional call to action, only where it fits naturally

Rules for the angles:

- Every idea must come from something specific: a question a customer
  actually asked, a mistake you see people make, a number, a before and
  after, a thing that surprised you. "Five tips for productivity" is not
  an angle.
- No more than two of the seven may be promotional.
- At least two must be usable without any preparation — something that
  can be written in ten minutes on a bad day.
- Do not repeat an angle from the recent list at the bottom. If the
  content doc is connected, read the last four weeks from it instead and
  avoid repeating anything there.

Then, separately, list three ideas that are too big for a single post and
would work better as a longer piece. One line each. These are for later,
not for next week.

Email the plan to the connected account, subject "Content week of [date]".
Lay it out day by day so it can be read on a phone.

Write the opening lines in the voice described below. If you are unsure
whether a line sounds like me, choose the plainer version.

--- EDIT BELOW THIS LINE ---

Everything in [square brackets] below is an example. Replace it with
your own and delete the brackets. If anything is still in brackets when
this runs, it is not a real setting — ignore it and say so at the top of
your output rather than treating the example as an instruction.

What I do: [e.g. I help small workshops stop running on spreadsheets]
Who I am talking to: [be specific — "people who run a shop and do their own books on a Sunday"]
What I want them to eventually do: [e.g. book a call, or start a trial]
Three things I believe that most people in my field do not: [e.g. 1) most software is bought to avoid a conversation 2) onboarding fails in week three, not week one 3) price is almost never the real objection]
Words and phrases I would never use: [e.g. leverage, synergy, "excited to share", "in today’s fast-paced world"]

Weekly shape:
  Monday — short post
  Tuesday — short post
  Wednesday — long post
  Thursday — video
  Friday — short post
  Saturday — nothing
  Sunday — email

Angles already used recently (add to this as you go): [paste last week’s angles here, or connect the content log so it remembers for you]
```

## Set up the content doc (optional, but it gets much better)

Make one document in Google Drive called something like `Content log`. Each week, paste the plan into the top of it.

Connect Google Drive to the routine and add one line to the instructions: *"Read the content log document before planning, and avoid repeating anything from the last four weeks."*

The difference is real. Without the log, the routine is smart but forgetful, and by week five it starts circling. With it, it builds on what you have already said.

## Before your first run

**The bottom section is the whole routine.** "Three things I believe that most people in my field do not" is the one that separates a usable plan from generic filler. Spend five minutes on it; you will use it for a year.

**Change the weekly shape to your real life.** Seven days of content is a default, not a prescription. If you post three times a week, delete four rows. A plan that assumes more than you will do gets abandoned in a fortnight.

**Friday afternoon is deliberate.** You read it while the week is still fresh, and Monday starts with the decision already made. Sunday evening works too. Monday morning does not — by then you needed it an hour ago.

## What a good run looks like

> **Monday — short post**
> Angle: the question three different customers asked this month, which nobody asks out loud.
> Opening: *"Nobody wants to admit they have no idea what their best-selling product actually costs them."*
> Leave them believing: the number is knowable, and knowing it changes decisions.
>
> **Wednesday — long post**
> Angle: the before and after of one specific change, with the real figures.
> Opening: *"We changed one line on the checkout page. Here is what it did."*
>
> **Thursday — video**
> Angle: show the thing rather than describe it. Screen recording, two minutes, no intro.
> Opening: *"This takes about ninety seconds, so I will just do it."*
>
> **Too big for a post, save for later**
> — the full teardown of why most onboarding emails go unread
> — a walkthrough of the first ninety days, with the mistakes left in

## Prefer Slack instead of email?

Plenty of teams plan content in Slack rather than an inbox.

In the instructions, replace the "Email the plan" sentence with:

> Post the plan as a single message to the #content channel in Slack.

Change `#content` to the channel you actually use, then swap the **Gmail**
connector for **Slack**. Nothing else changes.

## When it goes wrong

**Every idea sounds like every other creator.** The bottom section is empty or vague. "Who I am talking to" doing the heavy lifting: "small business owners" is not an answer, "people who run a shop and do their own books on a Sunday" is.

**It keeps suggesting the same three angles.** You are not feeding the recent list, or the content doc is not connected. This is the single most common complaint and it has exactly one cause.

**The opening lines are too clever.** Add to the voice section: "Write the way you would say it to one person across a table. No wordplay in the first line."

**It writes whole posts.** Add: "Give the opening line only. Do not write the body."
