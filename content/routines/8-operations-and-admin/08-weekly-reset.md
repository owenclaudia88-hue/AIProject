# Weekly Reset

Friday afternoon: what got done, what did not, and the one thing that keeps not happening.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Runs** | Fridays, 16:30 |
| **Cron** | `30 16 * * 5` |
| **Connectors** | Gmail, Google Calendar. Stripe and Google Drive optional |
| **Takes** | One run a week |

---

## The week nobody reviews

Weeks disappear. You finish one with a vague sense of having been busy and no clear idea what actually moved, which means you start the next one from the same place — reacting to whatever is loudest.

A proper weekly review fixes this and takes about forty minutes, which is why roughly nobody does it on a Friday afternoon.

This routine does the reconstruction part, which is most of the forty minutes. You get the week in front of you at 16:30 on Friday and the only thing left to do is the part that needs you: deciding what next week is for.

It is the last routine to set up and the one that ties the others together.

## Set it up

1. **New routine**, name it `Weekly Reset`
2. Paste the instructions and **fill in the bottom section**
3. **Schedule** → **Weekly** → Friday, 16:30
4. **Connectors**: Gmail and Google Calendar. Add Stripe and Google Drive if you have them
5. **Create**

## Instructions

```
Review the week and set up the next one.

Read the last 7 days of email, calendar and, if connected, Stripe and
the Drive folders listed at the bottom. Also read last week's version of
this review if you can find it in email — comparing week to week is
where most of the value is.

Seven sections, and keep the whole thing under 400 words.

1. WHAT ACTUALLY HAPPENED. The five or six things that genuinely moved
   this week. Concrete, with the day. Not a list of activity — a list of
   things that are now different.

2. THE NUMBERS. If Stripe is connected: revenue, payments, new
   customers, against last week. Four lines maximum. No commentary
   unless something moved by more than the threshold at the bottom.

3. WHERE THE TIME WENT. From the calendar: hours in meetings, number of
   meetings, and how many hours were unscheduled. Then, honestly,
   whether that matches what section 1 says got done. A week of 22
   meeting-hours and two things moved is a finding, and it is one that
   only appears when you put those two numbers next to each other.

4. WHAT DID NOT HAPPEN. Anything named as a plan for this week — in
   last week's review, in email, in the calendar — that has no evidence
   of being done. Be direct. This section is the reason to run this.

5. STILL OPEN. Anything promised to somebody and not delivered, and
   anybody waiting on a reply for more than three days. Who, what, how
   long.

6. THE ONE THING THAT KEEPS NOT HAPPENING. Anything that has now been in
   section 4 for two or more weeks running. Name it, say how many weeks,
   and say nothing else about it. It either needs to be done next week
   or dropped on purpose, and both are fine — drifting is the only bad
   option.

7. NEXT WEEK. What is already in the calendar, anything with a deadline,
   and the three things that would make next week count. Three, in
   order, each one line.

Then one closing line: the single most useful thing that happened this
week. One line, no commentary.

Email it to the connected account, subject "Week of [date]".

Be direct in sections 4 and 6. Do not soften them, do not add
encouragement, and do not explain away a bad week. A review that makes
every week sound fine is a review that is not worth reading.

--- EDIT BELOW THIS LINE ---

Everything in [square brackets] below is an example. Replace it with
your own and delete the brackets. If anything is still in brackets when
this runs, it is not a real setting — ignore it and say so at the top of
your output rather than treating the example as an instruction.

What this quarter is for: [e.g. getting to 200 paying customers without hiring]
Revenue change worth commenting on: 25%
Drive folders with my plans or notes: [e.g. Planning, Weekly notes]
Things that are always in the calendar and are not work: [e.g. school run, gym, lunch, recurring personal appointments]
```

## Before your first run

**Section 6 is the whole routine.** Everything that quietly kills a quarter is something that was going to get done every week for eleven weeks. Naming it, with a count, is uncomfortable in exactly the way that works.

**Section 3 puts two numbers next to each other that nobody ever compares.** Meeting hours and things moved. Most people have never seen those two figures in the same place, and the first time is usually instructive.

**Let it be blunt.** The instruction against softening is there because the temptation to edit it out after a bad week is real. A review that always says you did well is a diary.

**Friday at 16:30.** Late enough to be a review, early enough to do something about section 5 before the weekend.

**It reads its own previous output**, so the first two or three runs are weaker than the ones after. Section 4 and section 6 only work once there is a previous week to compare against.

## What a good run looks like

> **Week of 22 September**
>
> **What happened**
> — New pricing page live, Wednesday.
> — Supplier contract signed, Thursday.
> — Northfield Makes verbally agreed, Thursday — £2,400.
> — Twelve support threads cleared.
>
> **Numbers** — £2,140, 43 payments, 6 new customers. Last week: £1,980, 41, 5. Nothing to comment on.
>
> **Where the time went** — 17 hours in 14 meetings. 9 hours unscheduled across the week. Four things moved, and two of them were on Thursday.
>
> **What did not happen**
> — The card machine decision. Third week.
> — The migration breakdown for Priya, promised Monday, still not sent.
> — The customer survey, planned Monday, not started.
>
> **Still open**
> — Priya Shah waiting 4 days for the migration breakdown.
> — Sarah L. waiting 5 days about a double charge. Has written three times.
> — You owe Marcus the forecast volumes, promised on the 10th.
>
> **The one thing that keeps not happening**
> The card machine decision. Three weeks.
>
> **Next week**
> Calendar: quarter end Friday, supplier call Tuesday, two people away Thursday and Friday.
> 1. Sarah L.'s double charge. It becomes a chargeback if it waits again.
> 2. The card machine. Decide it or drop it.
> 3. Priya's migration breakdown — it is the last thing blocking £2,400.
>
> *Most useful thing this week: Northfield Makes said yes, and they said yes because the migration cost was quoted honestly rather than minimised.*

## Prefer Slack instead of email?

In the instructions, replace the "Email it" sentence with:

> Post the review as a single message to my Slack direct messages.

Add the **Slack** connector. Keep Gmail as a source — it is still what gets read.

## When it goes wrong

**Section 4 is always empty.** It cannot find where you write plans down. Point it at the folder, or at last week's review, and make sure section 7 of that review is where your plans actually live.

**It is encouraging.** Harden it: "No encouragement, no praise, no framing of a bad week as a learning opportunity. State what happened."

**It is too long.** The 400-word limit is doing work. If it drifts, add: "If you exceed 400 words, cut section 1 first."

**Section 6 names the same thing forever.** That is correct behaviour and an accurate report. Decide it or drop it — the routine will keep asking until one of those happens.

**The three things for next week are just the three unfinished things.** Sometimes right, often not. Add: "At least one of the three must be something that moves this quarter forward rather than something left over."
