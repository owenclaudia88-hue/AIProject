# Customer Language Miner

The words your customers use, collected, so you can stop writing in your own.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Runs** | Fridays, 17:00 |
| **Cron** | `0 17 * * 5` |
| **Connectors** | Gmail. Google Drive recommended |
| **Takes** | One run a week |

---

## You do not talk like your customers

You have been inside this business for years. You use the internal word for the thing. You describe the problem the way it looks from your side, which is the side that already understands it.

Your customers describe the same problem completely differently, and their version is the version they type into a search box, say to a colleague, and recognise on a landing page. Every time you write in your language instead of theirs, you lose a few people who had the exact problem you solve and did not realise it.

The fix is not creativity. It is collection. Every week your customers hand you the right words in emails, support threads, and reviews. This routine gathers them and keeps them in one place.

## Set it up

1. In Google Drive, make a document called `Customer language`
2. **New routine**, name it `Customer Language Miner`
3. Paste the instructions and **fill in the bottom section**
4. **Schedule** → **Weekly** → Friday, 17:00
5. **Connectors**: Gmail and Google Drive
6. **Create**

## Instructions

```
Collect the words our customers and prospects used this week.

Read all email from the last 7 days, in both directions, excluding the
senders, domains and labels at the bottom, and excluding anything we
wrote.

Pull out and quote exactly — never paraphrase, because the paraphrase is
the thing being avoided:

1. HOW THEY DESCRIBE THE PROBLEM. Every phrase somebody used for the
   thing we solve, before we gave them our words for it. First messages
   from new enquiries are the richest source here, because they have not
   read our site properly yet.

2. HOW THEY DESCRIBE THE RESULT. What changed for them, in their words.
   These are the sentences that belong on a sales page.

3. THE WORDS THEY USE FOR THINGS. Any place where they have a different
   name for something than we do. Put their word and ours side by side.

4. WHAT THEY WORRY ABOUT. Hesitations, caveats, "as long as", "the only
   thing is", "I just need to check". These are objections in their
   natural state, before anybody made them defensive.

5. WHAT THEY COMPARE US TO. Anything they mention as the alternative,
   including doing nothing, doing it by hand, and a spreadsheet — which
   is the real competitor far more often than another company is.

6. METAPHORS AND OFF-HAND PHRASES. "Falling over", "held together with
   string", "the Sunday night job". These are the most useful lines of
   all and the most likely to be smoothed away if you summarise.

For each quote give the date and whether it was a customer, a prospect
or somebody who did not buy. Do not give names — this document is about
language, not people.

Then two short observations:

- Any phrase that appeared more than once this week, from different
  people. Repeated language is the language to use.
- Any place where their word and our word differ, and our word appears
  on our own website. That is a specific, fixable thing.

Append this to the Google Doc named below, under a heading with this
week's date. Do not rewrite or reorganise what is already in the
document.

Then email me only the two observations and the five best quotes. The
full collection lives in the document; the email is the part I will
actually read.

Never tidy a quote. Keep the typos, the lower case, the run-on
sentences. The imperfections are what make it sound like a person, and
sounding like a person is the entire point.

--- EDIT BELOW THIS LINE ---

Everything in [square brackets] below is an example. Replace it with
your own and delete the brackets. If anything is still in brackets when
this runs, it is not a real setting — ignore it and say so at the top of
your output rather than treating the example as an instruction.

Document to append to: Customer language
Our own email addresses and domains: [e.g. @yourcompany.com, you@gmail.com if you use it for work]
Ignore these senders, domains and labels: [e.g. @yourcompany.com, noreply@, newsletters, label:Receipts]
The internal words we use that customers might not: [e.g. we say "reporting cycle", they say "month end"]
```

## What to do with the document

After a couple of months you will have a file of hundreds of real customer sentences. Open it whenever you have to write anything that faces a customer:

- sales page headlines, taken from group 2
- the FAQ, taken from group 4
- ad copy, taken from group 1 and group 6
- the comparison page, taken from group 5
- search terms and page titles, taken from group 3

Writing from this file is a completely different job from writing from scratch. You are choosing between real sentences rather than inventing plausible ones.

## Before your first run

**Never let it tidy the quotes.** The instruction is emphatic because the temptation is strong and the tidied version is worthless. "its falling over now there are four of us" is the useful sentence. "Our current process no longer scales with our team size" is what everybody's website already says.

**First messages from enquiries are the gold.** Before somebody has read your site, they describe the problem in their own terms. After, they describe it in yours. Both are in your inbox; only the first is useful.

**The email is short on purpose.** The document is the asset, and a weekly email containing 60 quotes would not get read. Five and two observations will.

**Group 6 is the one to watch.** Metaphors are what people remember and repeat. They are also the first casualty of any summarising.

## What a good run looks like

> **This week's two observations**
>
> **Repeated, from three different people:** some version of *"falling over"*. Used by two prospects and one customer, independently. Your site says "scales with your team". Nobody has ever said "scales" to you.
>
> **Their word vs yours:** they say **"the Sunday job"** and **"month end"**. Your site says **"reporting cycle"**, which appears four times on the homepage. Nobody uses that phrase.
>
> **Five best quotes this week**
>
> *"its falling over now theres four of us all in the same sheet"* — prospect, Tuesday
> *"I got my sundays back which sounds dramatic but its true"* — customer, Wednesday
> *"honestly we were just doing it by hand, there wasnt a system"* — prospect, Thursday
> *"the only thing is I need to know I can get my data out if we stop"* — prospect, Thursday
> *"its held together with string and im the string"* — customer, Friday
>
> *Full collection appended to Customer language.*

## No Google Drive?

It works without it — drop the appending instruction and have it email the whole collection. You lose the accumulation, which is most of the value, so keep your own document and paste the emails into it if Drive is not an option.

## When it goes wrong

**It paraphrases everything.** The most common failure and the one that empties the routine of value. Harden it: "Every item must be inside quotation marks and must be reproduced character for character, including mistakes. If you cannot quote it exactly, leave it out."

**It quotes your own team.** Your domain is not in the bottom section.

**Everything is from support threads.** Those skew towards problems. If your enquiries arrive through a form rather than email, point it at that label too, or the collection will be all complaints and no results.

**Nothing in group 6.** Metaphors are genuinely rare — a couple a month is normal. If there are none for two months, check that it is not tidying them into plain language.
